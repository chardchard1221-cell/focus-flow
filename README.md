## 🌌 Overview

Fast-Text is a desktop-based **pygame reading and focus trainer** that flips the traditional "read more, read faster" advice on its head. Instead of bombarding you with walls of text, it slices, paces, and rhythmically delivers words in micro-bursts calibrated to your current cognitive ceiling — then gently stretches that ceiling upward.

Where most speed-reading tools treat the reader as a machine to be optimized, Fast-Text treats the reader as a musician learning an instrument. Every session is a rehearsal. Every drill is a bar of music. And over days and weeks, your eyes, your inner voice, and your attention span begin to play in harmony instead of fighting for the conductor's baton.

The project began as a personal experiment: can a small pygame window train a person to read faster *without* losing comprehension? The answer, after months of iteration, is a confident yes — when the training is structured like a sport rather than a lecture.

## 🎯 The Philosophy Behind Fast-Text

Reading fast is not about moving your eyes quicker. It is about removing the friction that makes slow reading feel normal:

- **Subvocalization** — the inner voice that reads every word aloud in your head.
- **Regression** — the habit of jumping backward to "double-check" a sentence you already understood.
- **Attention drift** — the silent moment your mind leaves the page and wanders into tomorrow's to-do list.

Fast-Text attacks all three with dedicated micro-drills, wrap-up reflection prompts, and a focus timer that penalizes wandering without ever feeling punitive. Think of it less as a productivity app and more as a **gym for the reading brain** — complete with warm-ups, intervals, and cool-downs.

## ⚙️ How It Works

Fast-Text operates as a layered pipeline:

1. **Input Layer** — Accepts plain text files, pasted passages, or built-in drill texts.
2. **Segmentation Layer** — Splits the passage into rhythm units based on punctuation and clause boundaries.
3. **Pacing Layer** — Assigns a delivery interval to each unit, informed by your rolling comprehension score.
4. **Presentation Layer** — Renders each unit on a distraction-free canvas inside a pygame window.
5. **Evaluation Layer** — After each drill, prompts a short recall or summary task and scores it.
6. **Feedback Layer** — Feeds the score back into the pacing engine and updates your telemetry dashboard.

Because each layer is independent, contributors can extend Fast-Text with new segmentation rules, new presentation themes, or entirely new evaluation modes without touching the rest of the pipeline.

## 🌐 Multilingual & Accessibility Layer

Fast-Text was built from the ground up with the assumption that not every reader reads left-to-right, space-separated English. The segmentation engine handles:

- **Space-delimited languages** (English, Spanish, French, German, and many more).
- **Character-dense scripts** (Chinese, Japanese, Korean) with configurable chunk widths.
- **Right-to-left scripts** (Arabic, Hebrew, Persian) with mirrored rendering.
- **Diacritic-heavy scripts** with font fallback chains.

Accessibility features include high-contrast themes, adjustable font scaling, reduced-motion mode, and a screen-reader-friendly summary export of every completed session.

## 📱 Responsive Interface Design

Although Fast-Text is a desktop application, its layout engine is deliberately responsive. The interface detects the current window dimensions and rearranges panels accordingly:

- **Compact mode** hides the telemetry sidebar and focuses purely on the training canvas.
- **Standard mode** presents the training canvas alongside a slim stats column.
- **Wide mode** opens the full dashboard with drill library, history, and settings visible at once.

The result is an experience that feels native on a tiny netbook and cinematic on a triple-monitor battlestation.
